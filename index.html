Know Your Format

Scoring DNA: This League Is Not Standard

Rule number one of winning a league: know which rules are quietly rigged. Big Ballers scoring has three structural tilts that should drive every start/sit and trade this season.

1. Rushing Is Overpaid — On Purpose

Rushing yards score 0.1/yd vs. 0.05/yd passing — double the rate. Add +1 for a 100-yard rushing game, +2 for 200, and +2/+3 bonuses on 40- and 50-yard rushing TDs, while receiving gets no equivalent game bonus. Translation: bell-cow RBs and rushing QBs (Allen, Lamar, Daniels, Hurts, Maye) carry a hidden premium over their standard-league value. Teams built on them — Saquon my Dak, Bijan’s Mustard — are playing the format.

2. Pocket Passers Get Taxed

At 1 point per 20 passing yards, a 300-yard game is worth just 15 yards’ worth of rushing production — and the -0.5 per sack quietly bleeds QBs behind bad lines. A 5-point passing TD softens it, but volume passers without legs need multi-score games to keep pace. Delhi Belly’s Goff-only plan and McDonkey’s Stafford-only plan both live on the wrong side of this math.

3. Kickers and Defenses Actually Matter Here

Distance-tiered kicking (5 points for 50+ and 60+, -1 on misses) makes a big-leg kicker worth roughly 2–3 points a week over a checkdown leg. D/ST scoring stacks points-allowed tiers and yardage tiers and 6-point return TDs — elite defenses can post WR2 numbers. This is the only honest defense of Quarterback Sackgood taking Aubrey (7.07) and the Broncos (8.06) five rounds early. It’s still expensive; it’s just not insane.

Deep Cuts from Nine Seasons

The Iron Man Without a Second Ring

Spencer Popp is one of only three managers to play all nine seasons (with Sean Kramer and Rohan Sarode — the last founding members standing) and has posted eight consecutive winning seasons since 2018. One title to show for it: 2019, as Thomas and Friends. The 71-51 career record says the program works; the trophy case says the playoffs haven't cooperated since.

Best Record, No Ring

Phil Wilkinson's 11-2 in 2020 is still the best regular season in league history — and it ended in a fourth-place finish. Eight seasons in, Wilkinson has zero titles and zero podiums: the largest body of good work with the least hardware in the league.

The Verber Curve

Jeff Verber went 4-10 in his second season (2021) — then rattled off 11-3, 10-4, 10-4, 11-3, with two titles and a league-best .663 career win percentage. The three-peat bid isn't a hot streak; it's a five-year regime.

The Kramer Whiplash

Sean Kramer owns the widest range in league history: a 1-12 season in 2019, a championship in 2021, runner-up in 2024. Career record 55-67 — below .500 with a ring, proof the playoffs care nothing for your regular season.

The Departed Dynasty

Keith Gardiner leaves the league tied for the most titles ever (2020, 2022) after nine seasons and a 58-64 career record — the only multi-time champ with a losing record, and the biggest trophy case ever to walk out the door.

The Basement Record

Areg A's 0-13 in 2017 remains the only winless season in league history, at 76.7 points per game. One season, zero wins, immortality of a kind. Honorable mention: Kramer and Robert Dowdall both survived 1-12 campaigns.

Make Arlington took the highest-drafted NFL rookie RB since Saquon (3rd overall, $50.5M guaranteed) — while he nurses a high-ankle sprain, and while a rival rosters his direct handcuff (Allgeier, 15.02, Bijan’s Mustard). Ceiling: league-winner. Floor: September regret.

Eating W’s is attempting a three-peat with exactly two rostered running backs in a 2-RB-starter league. Either the back-to-back champ sees something the board doesn’t, or the waiver wire is about to become their third RB.
